\abstract{\textbf{Background:} Text can provide target and spatial context for medical image segmentation, but augmentation can invalidate spatial language and target-derived prompts can exaggerate meaningful text use. Accuracy and prompt reliability therefore require separate controls.

\textbf{Methods:} \methodname{} combines a frozen pooled BioMedCLIP adapter, two forward-active projector ATConv replacements, and a deterministic image--mask--text plan. Protocol V3 fixed manifests, split roles, mask rules, and evaluation; the richer shared plan was a separately frozen amendment. Direction phrases are rewritten for flips and right-angle rotations; an exact no-rewrite control receives identical image--mask transforms. Five public benchmarks used three seeds, native-grid scoring, a retrospective 90-cell ablation, correct/shuffled/fixed/empty prompt controls, and a post hoc presence/class-stratified derangement.

\textbf{Results:} \methodname{} achieved Public-5 macro Dice of $87.87\%$ and IoU of $80.97\%$. Versus the horizontal-flip Base reference, Dice increased by $1.46$ points (95\% dataset-stratified bootstrap confidence interval $0.95$ to $1.99$) and IoU by $1.74$ points (95\% interval $1.13$ to $2.34$). The shared-plan no-rewrite row was numerically best at $87.96\%$ Dice; rewriting changed Dice by $-0.09$ points (95\% interval $-0.30$ to $0.10$). Base plus the shared plan reached $87.83\%$, leaving $0.04$ points for the complete model (95\% interval $-0.40$ to $0.41$). Case-specific versus constant-text training differed by $1.90$ points (95\% interval $1.16$ to $2.75$). True text exceeded presence/class-stratified derangement by $+5.63$ Dice points (95\% interval $+4.71$ to $+6.67$), versus $+14.66$ under unstratified shuffling.

\textbf{Conclusions:} Most macro improvement is attributable to the shared training policy; additional macro accuracy from rewriting or the BioMedCLIP--ATConv stack was not established. Presence/class matching explains part of the shuffled-prompt gap, but the residual remains privileged because public prompts contain mask- or label-derived attributes. The results support a reliability-audited benchmark, not clinical-report understanding or deployment generalization.}
