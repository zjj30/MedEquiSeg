\section{Background}
\label{sec:intro}
Vision--language pretraining has made free-text conditioning practical for dense
prediction \cite{radford2021clip,clipseg,biomedclip}. In medical segmentation,
language can identify a target, describe morphology, or provide spatial context
\cite{lvit,causalclipseg,medclipseg}. This additional modality also creates two
reliability requirements that do not exist in image-only segmentation: training
transforms must preserve the joint meaning of the image, mask, and prompt, and
the resulting prediction should depend on the case-matched prompt when that
prompt is claimed to provide useful guidance.

Conventional geometric augmentation applies one transform to the image and mask
but leaves text unchanged \cite{shorten2019survey}. If a prompt locates a lesion
in the image frame, a horizontal flip changes \textit{left} to \textit{right},
a vertical flip changes \textit{upper} to \textit{lower}, and a right-angle
rotation changes both axes. The transformed mask remains geometrically valid,
yet the conditioning text may become false. This is an avoidable cross-modal
supervision conflict. Separately, a geometrically valid prompt can still be
uninformative or weakly grounded; high segmentation accuracy alone cannot show
that the text branch is being used. The relevant augmentation example is
image-frame location, rather than
anatomical laterality: mirroring a breast image does not change the patient's
anatomical left or right.

We therefore treat the shared training policy as the main empirical object and
prompt reliability as a control on how its accuracy is obtained. \methodname{}
combines biomedical text features, adaptive convolution, and synchronized
image--mask--text augmentation, while matched and fixed-checkpoint controls
prevent the aggregate result from being misattributed to rewriting or to
clinically meaningful language understanding. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A complete retrospective 90-cell ordered component ablation that
  separates the base pathway, frozen pooled BioMedCLIP embedding adapter, the
  effective two-projector ATConv adapter, the shared training plan, and prompt
  rewriting.
  \item A same-data-and-evaluation-protocol comparison on five public datasets,
  with three training seeds, restored native-grid mask scoring, and three
  text-guided and six image-only architectures.
  \item A protocol and prompt-reliability audit comprising an exact-plan
  no-rewrite control, correct/shuffled/fixed/empty inference controls,
  a post hoc presence/class-stratified prompt derangement, constant-text and
  Base-plus-plan follow-ups, prompt-provenance boundaries, and a runtime check
  of which registered ATConv modules enter the forward graph.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related work}
\subsubsection{Text-guided segmentation}
CLIP learns a transferable joint image--text space \cite{radford2021clip}, and
CLIPSeg adapts it to dense prediction through a lightweight conditional decoder
\cite{clipseg}. ViLT shows that joint vision--language interaction can be built
without a region detector or convolutional visual front end \cite{vilt}, while
BioMedCLIP introduces biomedical image--text pretraining at scale
\cite{biomedclip}. LViT integrates language with a transformer--UNet pathway
\cite{lvit}; CausalCLIPSeg uses causal intervention for referring medical
segmentation \cite{causalclipseg}; and MedCLIPSeg uses probabilistic cross-modal
adaptation \cite{medclipseg}. TextMatch studies multimodal consistency in
semi-supervised segmentation \cite{textmatch}, whereas ARSeg targets missing or
imbalanced textual attributes \cite{arseg}. These models motivate a controlled
test of whether the training prompt still describes the transformed image.

Recent work also separates prompt content from fusion design. DescriptorMedSAM
evaluates a structured progression from target name to name--shape--location
descriptors and finds that shape and location provide complementary guidance
\cite{descriptormedsam}. Spatial-Aware Symmetric Alignment encodes directional
constraints as region-level guidance while aligning descriptive and diagnostic
expressions \cite{ssa}. NTP-MRISeg instead casts image, text, and mask tokens as
one autoregressive sequence \cite{ntpmriseg}. These architecture- and
descriptor-level advances are complementary to \methodname{}, which asks both
whether a supplied spatial descriptor remains valid after training-time geometry
and whether the trained prediction remains case-specifically text dependent.

\subsubsection{Image-only reference models}
U-Net \cite{unet}, U-Net++ \cite{unetpp}, nnU-Net \cite{nnunet}, Rolling-Unet \cite{rollingunet},
U-Mamba \cite{umamba}, and U-KAN \cite{ukan} demonstrate that a strong visual
pathway can perform well without text. A multimodal method should therefore be
compared against both text-guided baselines and competitive image-only models.
RollingUNet, U-Net, U-Net++, nnU-Net, U-Mamba, and U-KAN are complete for all
datasets and all three seeds under Protocol V3. This breadth is important
because the strongest image-only reference differs across datasets.

\subsubsection{Consistency under paired augmentation}
Data augmentation generally assumes that the target remains valid after a
transformation \cite{shorten2019survey}. For image--text learning, this assumption
extends to language. Yi et al. update predefined caption keywords when
horizontal flipping image--caption pairs and combine this with
semantics-preserving appearance augmentation \cite{augmentpairs}. In multimodal
medical segmentation, RBTCA instead grounds textual cues in image regions before
augmenting a modality-aware representation \cite{rbtca}. Chai et al. identify the same flip/rotation conflict and
avoid it by fusing text into a pseudo-image before augmentation
\cite{chai2025earlyfusion}. Semi-MedRef synchronizes positional spans during
patch mixing and perturbs position phrases in a semi-supervised teacher--student
setting \cite{semimedref}. \methodname{} therefore does not claim priority for the
general alignment problem, paired caption augmentation, or exchanging individual
left/right tokens. Its specific contribution is the cross-dataset evidence chain:
an explicit lexical map for a finite image-coordinate vocabulary, deterministic
exact-plan controls, a complete ordered ablation, and a privileged-prompt
reliability audit.

\subsection{Prompt-evidence scope and real-text validation}
The public MedCLIPSeg mask/label-derived captions are restricted to mechanism
analysis. They do not constitute real-text validation. Deployment-oriented
evidence, which is not available in the present study, would require blinded
clinician-authored or clinician-verified prompts generated from images without
access to masks. Such a study must freeze the vocabulary and annotation
instructions before opening the untouched test set and must compare correct,
shuffled, fixed, and empty prompts. We do not use machine-generated captions as
a substitute for clinical text evidence.

Spatial coverage counts prompts containing any member of the finite dictionary,
including center, middle, top, bottom, upper, lower, left, and right compounds.
This count alone overstates intervention frequency because central phrases can be
invariant and geometric operations are sampled probabilistically. We therefore
replay the exact keyed random generator for every training case, epoch, and seed
and report the realized proportion of events whose final prompt string differs
from the original. Across 100 epochs and seeds 123, 456, and 789, the rates
range from $5.75\pm0.04\%$ for BRISC to $45.75\pm0.06\%$ for COVID-19.
Complete event counts and operation rates are reported in Supplementary
Table~S7.

\subsection{Protocol V3}
The protocol identifier is \texttt{MEDSEG\_TEXT\_V3\_20260710}. Manifests are
fixed across seeds. Seeds 123, 456, and 789 change parameter initialization,
minibatch order, and augmentation sampling; they do not resplit the data.
Validation selects the best checkpoint, and the test split is accessed only by
the final prediction and evaluation scripts. Predictions are restored to the
native ground-truth resolution before scoring.

The protocol hash locks the dataset registry, manifests, split roles, mask
rules, seeds, threshold, and evaluation specification, but it does not encode
the full training policy or evaluator source. In particular, the original V3 LCAugv2 router restricted ClinicDB and
COVID-19 to horizontal flips and excluded affine, translation, and zoom. The
shared-plan rows in the 90-cell matrix instead use the separately dated R11
follow-up policy: all five public datasets receive the same multi-geometry,
appearance, mild affine, translation, and zoom sampler. That policy was frozen
before the paired R11/no-rewrite matrix, but it is an amendment on the fixed V3
data and evaluation protocol rather than part of the original protocol lock.
Accordingly, we use the V3 protocol hash only for data/evaluation identity and
retain a separate run-configuration hash, code hash, manifest hash, cache hash,
checkpoint hash, and original command log for each training task. We do not
describe the shared-plan matrix as prospectively confirmatory under the original
July 10 augmentation specification (Supplementary Table S11).

BRISC contains duplicated images with conflicting masks. Protocol V3 drops
conflicting duplicate groups while preserving exact holdout membership; this
sanitation rule is part of the dataset registry and protocol hash. The public
MedCLIPSeg manifests inherit the repository's
Train/Val/Test folders and do not expose a populated patient- or sequence-ID
field; consequently, we describe these as fixed image-level holdouts rather than
claiming patient-level separation. A post hoc filename audit can recover subject
IDs for part of COVID-19: no recovered subject crosses into the fixed test set,
but 434 subjects occur in both its original training and validation folders. We
therefore performed a separately hashed sensitivity rerun that assigns every
recoverable subject to only one training or validation split while preserving the
fixed test set. Rows without recoverable IDs remain case-level groups. The
grouped-validation training results are reported in Supplementary Table S10, and
the reported-checkpoint test-set cluster sensitivity additionally addresses
uncertainty from repeated test images.

We report Dice and IoU in the main table and additionally export precision,
recall, normalized surface Dice (NSD) at two pixels, HD95, and ASSD. A both-empty
case receives perfect overlap and zero distance. A one-sided empty case receives
zero overlap and infinite distance, and the number of such boundary failures is
reported with the distance metrics. Supplementary Tables S1--S5 report NSD,
finite-case HD95 and ASSD, and the one-empty failure rate for every method and
dataset. Finite distance means are interpreted only together with the failure
rate.

\subsection{MedEquiSeg framework}
\label{sec:method}
\subsubsection{Framework overview and reliability scope}
\methodname{} comprises a visual segmentation pathway, a frozen biomedical text
representation pathway, a shared transformation plan, and an evaluation-time
semantic audit (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}). The visual pathway fine-tunes the
CausalCLIPSeg RN50 encoder and retains its scale-wise supportive/inferior feature
partition, and two text-conditioned projection heads. Cached 512-dimensional
pooled BioMedCLIP features pass through a trainable state projection and condition
both heads; each evaluated head contains one active ATConv operator. During training, the shared plan
keeps the image and mask geometrically aligned and updates discrete spatial
phrases when their image-frame meaning changes. During evaluation, correct,
shuffled, fixed, and empty prompts test whether the checkpoint responds to
case-specific text. We use \emph{prompt-reliability-aware} in this operational
sense: the framework protects and audits prompt reliability. It does not estimate
per-case uncertainty or contain a learned reliability gate.

\subsubsection{Frozen follow-up shared plan}
The shared rows of the ordered ablation use the R11 follow-up policy defined
above. For case identifier $c$, epoch $e$, and training seed $s$, a keyed random
generator samples
\begin{equation}
  A_{c,e,s} = \mathcal{S}\!\left(H,V,R,F,Z,C,G,N\mid c,e,s\right),
\end{equation}
where $H$ and $V$ are horizontal and vertical flips, $R$ is a 90-degree
rotation, $F$ is a mild affine transform with translation, $Z$ is zoom, and
$C$, $G$, and $N$ denote color jitter, gamma, and Gaussian noise. The same
realized spatial plan is applied to image $x$ and mask $y$:
\begin{equation}
  x' = A^{x}_{c,e,s}(x), \qquad y' = A^{y}_{c,e,s}(y).
\end{equation}
Images use bilinear interpolation, masks use nearest-neighbor interpolation, and
the mask is never exposed to appearance operations. The training set is not
physically duplicated; a new deterministic plan is sampled online each epoch.

\subsubsection{Compositional direction rewriting}
Each recognized direction is mapped to a vector $d=(d_x,d_y)$ with components in
$\{-1,0,1\}$. Horizontal and vertical flips apply
\begin{equation}
  T_H(d_x,d_y)=(-d_x,d_y),\qquad
  T_V(d_x,d_y)=(d_x,-d_y),
\end{equation}
and a counter-clockwise 90-degree rotation applies
\begin{equation}
  T_R(d_x,d_y)=(d_y,-d_x).
\end{equation}
Sequential transforms compose these maps before converting the result back to
the original lexical family. Thus, \textit{upper right} remains an atomic target
rather than two unrelated replacements. Comma-separated targets are parsed as
separate atoms. The BioMedCLIP cache contains the finite lexical closure reachable
under all compositions, preventing an augmented prompt from silently falling
back to a missing embedding.

\subsubsection{Augmentation profile and scope}
\methodname{} uses one locked mild profile: $p_H=0.35$, $p_V=0.10$, and $p_R=0.12$;
when rotation is selected, $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, and $270^\circ$ are sampled
uniformly. Mild affine is selected with probability 0.15, with angle in
$[-5^\circ,5^\circ]$ and translation in $\pm2.5\%$ of image size. Zoom is
selected with probability 0.25 in $[1.00,1.07]$. Color jitter, gamma, and noise
have probabilities 0.45, 0.25, and 0.25, respectively; jitter magnitude is 0.05,
gamma is in $[0.90,1.12]$, and noise standard deviation is 0.006 after
normalization.

Only flips and right-angle rotations trigger exact direction rewriting. Small
affine rotations, translation, and off-center zoom do not exchange coordinate
axes, but they can move a target across a coarse location bin. Consequently,
\methodname{} guarantees lexical consistency for the discrete axis-changing
operations, not for arbitrary continuous spatial descriptions. The small ranges
bound this approximation. We additionally ran \locrecompute{}, a benchmark-only
diagnostic that recomputes coarse position phrases from the connected-component
centroids of the transformed training mask whenever affine, translation, or zoom
is sampled. Although it uses the same transform distribution, model family, and
training seeds, its augmentation seed key differs from the main run and the
realized per-case transforms are therefore not exactly paired. We report it only
as an exploratory same-distribution diagnostic.

\subsubsection{Multimodal segmentation pathway}
The evaluated model initializes the CausalCLIPSeg visual pathway from the official
OpenAI CLIP RN50 checkpoint and fine-tunes it end to end. Prompts are represented
by cached, frozen 512-dimensional pooled features from
\texttt{microsoft/BiomedCLIP-PubMedBERT\_256-vit\_base\_patch16\_224}. A trainable linear projection
maps each pooled feature to the 1024-dimensional state consumed by both
CausalCLIPSeg projectors. Each projector converts this state into a sample-specific
$3\times3$ kernel and bias. One same-channel convolution in each active projector
is replaced by ATConv \cite{atconv}, giving two effective ATConv operators in the
evaluated graph. The adapter also instantiates expanded token features, but all
token-level fusion switches are disabled in the reported configuration; those
tokens therefore do not enter the effective forward path. Plugin metadata records
four substitutions because two additional replacements target dormant legacy FPN
members that are not called by the released \texttt{FPN\_AD.forward}; we exclude
them from the architectural claim. A post-run forward-hook audit loaded the
reported BUSI seed-123 checkpoint and passed one real test case through the
model: both projector operators were called once with non-zero outputs, while
the two registered neck candidates were never called (Supplementary Table S11). Training uses a BCE--Dice objective with Dice
weight 0.5. Text conditions visual decoding but does not directly generate the
target mask. \norewrite{} has the identical architecture, objective, and sampled
image--mask plans; only prompt rewriting is disabled.

\subsection{Experimental design}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsubsection{Training and evaluation}
Inputs are resized to $224\times224$ for training. All \methodname{} runs use 100 epochs,
batch size 8, AdamW with initial learning rate $10^{-4}$ and weight decay
$10^{-4}$, and cosine decay to $10^{-6}$. Checkpoint selection uses validation
Dice. Evaluation uses a fixed threshold of 0.5. Predictions are restored from
the $224\times224$ inference grid to the native reference-mask grid before
scoring.
The principal \methodname{} and image-baseline environment used Python 3.11.15 and
PyTorch 2.5.1 with CUDA 12.1. The nnU-Net/MedCLIPSeg environment used
nnU-Net v2.8.0 and MONAI 1.3.0; the official U-Mamba path used Python 3.10.20
and nnU-Net v2.1.1. Runs used NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs with driver 580.119.02.
A package-level environment record lists the remaining core library versions.

Online complexity is measured from the frozen BUSI seed-123 checkpoints on one
NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 with batch size 1, FP32, and $224\times224$ inputs.
After model-specific warm-up, each forward pass is timed with wall-clock CUDA
synchronization; data loading and mask post-processing are excluded. The \methodname{}
and LViT measurements include cached-text lookup but not the one-time offline
BioMedCLIP or PubMedBERT encoding step. MedCLIPSeg instead runs its released
online UniMedCLIP/BiomedBERT path and is measured with both one probabilistic
sample and its official 30-sample test setting. Parameter counts include all
modules instantiated by each checkpoint loader and are not pruned to
output-connected subgraphs. Because the prompt-consistency policy is active only
during training, it adds no inference branch or parameter beyond the reported
\methodname{} backbone.

The expression \textit{same protocol} in this paper means the same locked
manifest, split roles, held-out test set, mask sanitation, prediction
restoration, and metric implementation. It does not imply that architectures
with different official recipes share identical losses, input sizes, or
augmentation. We therefore use the more precise phrase
\textit{same data and evaluation protocol} in comparative claims.

\subsubsection{Baselines and controls}
The completed comparison includes MedCLIPSeg, CausalCLIPSeg, LViT, RollingUNet,
U-Net, U-Net++, nnU-Net, U-Mamba, and U-KAN
\cite{medclipseg,causalclipseg,lvit,rollingunet,unet,unetpp,nnunet,umamba,ukan}.
The first three and \methodname{} are multimodal; the remaining six models are
image-only. Every method has three optimization seeds on every dataset. U-Net,
U-Net++, nnU-Net, U-Mamba, and U-KAN contribute 75 public
dataset--model--seed cells; RollingUNet contributes 15 more, for 90 image-only
public cells in total. The contextual CausalCLIPSeg row (internal run V3\_R0) and the
Base reference (V3\_ABL\_BASE) use the same CLIP-text/horizontal-flip recipe but
are independent three-seed training matrices; the latter is the reference for
the ordered component ablation, whereas the former is retained in the broad
model table.

\norewrite{} is the primary mechanism control. Four inference-time semantic controls are also
defined without retraining: correct case text, seed-fixed shuffled text,
a training-derived fixed prompt, and an empty prompt. Correct text must exceed
shuffled text in the Holm-adjusted paired rank screen before a dataset enters
the semantic-control visualization; a hierarchical interval excluding zero is
additionally required for the evidence to be described as robust to seed
uncertainty. The shuffled control applies a deterministic non-zero cyclic shift
(control seed 123) to the ordered test prompts. It preserves the complete prompt
multiset and assigns every row another row's prompt, although duplicated prompt
strings can remain textually unchanged; no class or attribute stratum is
preserved. To assess this confounding directly, a post hoc frozen-checkpoint
control permutes the complete prompt multiset separately within target-presence
and diagnostic-class strata. Strata are constructed only from released case
identifiers and prompt fields; the builder reads neither masks nor image pixels.
Within each stratum, a deterministic assignment maximizes changed prompt strings
while preserving one-to-one prompt closure. This changes 3,413 of 3,426 public
test prompts; 13 BUSI normal cases remain unchanged because every available
within-stratum prompt string is identical. Fixed and empty prompts are secondary
descriptive controls. The released target-derived captions on the
five public datasets define the formal but privileged referring benchmark.
Additional prompts generated directly from a case's mask for oracle diagnostic
experiments are a separate category and are excluded from formal performance
tables.

A frozen follow-up ordered component ablation uses the fixed V3 manifests and
evaluator for the base
CausalCLIPSeg pathway, BioMedCLIP substitution alone, ATConv alone, their
combination, the shared deterministic plan without rewriting, and the complete
\methodname{} configuration. Every configuration contains all five public
datasets and seeds 123, 456, and 789, yielding 90 unique
dataset--configuration--seed cells. The first four configurations use the same
hflip policy; the last two share the same richer online plan and differ only in
whether direction phrases are rewritten. Historical engineering runs using
other prompt-generation or evaluation definitions are excluded.

\subsubsection{Endpoints and statistical analysis}
The main accuracy-centered contrast reported in this retrospective ordered
ablation is the complete \methodname{} configuration minus the horizontal-flip Base
reference in equal-dataset Public-5 macro Dice. This contrast was selected after
the original frozen promotion gate and is therefore interpreted as post hoc
rather than as a prospectively declared primary endpoint. The mechanism
contrast frozen before completion of the paired matrix is \methodname{} minus \norewrite{} under an exactly matched
visual transformation plan. The ordered ablation additionally reports the
incremental BioMedCLIP and ATConv configurations. Both contrasts use
case-first paired inference after averaging the three seed values per case.
Dataset-specific Dice and IoU differences
and boundary measures are secondary endpoints. The primary semantic-control
endpoint is true versus shuffled Dice; fixed and empty controls are descriptive.
The presence/class-stratified derangement is a post hoc sensitivity analysis of
the true--shuffled contrast, not a replacement primary endpoint. It uses the
same case-first three-seed reduction, 10,000 paired bootstrap replicates, and
five-dataset Holm family as the other semantic comparisons.
Cross-model and cross-modality comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:bspc_v3_models}
are contextual rather than confirmatory because architectures and input
information differ.

Two additional training controls were completed after the 90-cell matrix. A
constant-text run keeps the complete recipe and shared plan but replaces every
cached prompt feature with the same empty-prompt embedding throughout training
and test. A Base-plus-plan run applies the richer shared plan to the Base-reference
recipe without BioMedCLIP substitution, ATConv, or rewriting. Their comparisons
with the earlier matrix are post hoc sensitivity analyses because the runner
code snapshot changed between batches, although manifests, evaluator, epochs,
optimizer settings, and command logs are retained. We therefore use these
controls to assess attribution, not as new confirmatory endpoints.

Seed-level results are reported as mean and sample standard deviation. For
hypothesis tests, per-case metrics are first averaged across seeds for each
method, then compared with paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests; paired $t$-tests are
reported as sensitivity analyses. Family-wise multiplicity is controlled with
Holm correction \cite{holm}. Confidence intervals use 10,000 paired-case
bootstrap replicates after the three seed values for each case have been
averaged. For the Public-5 macro effect, resampling is stratified within each
dataset before the five dataset-level effects are averaged, so each benchmark
receives equal weight. Training-seed variability is retained in the reported
mean and sample standard deviation but is not treated as an additional set of
independent cases. Bootstrap intervals estimate the mean effect, whereas the
Wilcoxon test targets paired rank differences; the two summaries are interpreted
together rather than treated as interchangeable significance decisions.
Case-level tests use manifest rows as the paired units and are interpreted
subject to the patient-grouping limitation described below. Because the
COVID-19 test set contains repeated images from 474 recoverable subjects, a
post hoc sensitivity analysis retains the image-weighted point estimate but
resamples subjects and training seeds for the interval and applies paired tests
to equal-subject mean differences. This clustered analysis is reported
separately and does not replace the pre-specified image-level analysis.

\section{Discussion}
The principal empirical result is higher accuracy for the complete configuration
than for the base multimodal pathway: $+1.46$ Dice pp (95\% CI $+0.95$ to
$+1.99$) and $+1.74$ IoU pp (95\% CI $+1.13$ to $+2.34$) in the Public-5
ordered ablation. Because the contrast was selected retrospectively and the
shared policy was a follow-up amendment on the fixed V3 manifests and evaluator,
it supports an association on this benchmark panel rather than a prospectively
confirmed universal effect. \methodname{} also has the highest equal-dataset
macro Dice and IoU among the reproduced models, but this does not mean that it
is the best method on every dataset.
The strongest image-only result is higher on ClinicDB, BUS-BRA, and BRISC,
whereas \methodname{} is higher on BUSI and COVID-19. Reporting both macro and
per-dataset results is therefore necessary: the macro captures consistency
across the benchmark panel, while the dataset table exposes modality-specific
failure and competitive margins.

The component study localizes this gain. BioMedCLIP and ATConv individually
change the Public-5 macro by only $+0.26$ and $+0.22$ Dice pp relative to the
base pathway; using both gives $+0.62$ pp. The shared plan without rewriting
reaches $+1.55$ pp, while the complete configuration reaches $+1.46$ pp. The
post hoc Base-plus-plan control independently reaches $+1.42$ pp, while the
complete-model residual over that control is $+0.04$ pp (95\% CI $-0.40$ to
$+0.41$). The shared policy therefore explains nearly all of the observed macro
point improvement; the data do not establish an additional aggregate accuracy
benefit from the complete BioMedCLIP--ATConv--rewrite stack beyond that policy.
The runtime audit confirms that ATConv was not globally disabled: two projector
operators execute and produce non-zero outputs. It also confirms that the other
two registered candidates are dormant, so these results support only the
effective two-projector adapter and not an ATConv4 claim.

Prompt reliability remains an interpretive control rather than the primary
performance claim. A shared plan guarantees image--mask alignment; the language
map addresses prompt validity under a subset of spatial transforms; and semantic
controls test whether the checkpoint depends on case-matched text. The exact-plan
control is essential because comparison with an unaugmented baseline cannot
distinguish language consistency from ordinary regularization. Likewise,
segmentation accuracy without shuffled or empty prompts cannot establish reliable
text use.

The semantic-control pattern constrains interpretation. Large true--shuffled
gaps on all five public sets show that prompt identity affects the prediction.
After matching target presence and diagnostic class, the Public-5 gap falls from
$+14.66$ to $+5.63$ Dice pp. This attenuation identifies coarse class/presence
confounding in the unstratified control, while the remaining effect is
concentrated in BUSI and COVID-19 and is nearly absent on BUS-BRA. The residual
therefore supports sensitivity to privileged case-specific prompt content, not
a general claim of language grounding.
The constant-text training control also differs from the complete model by
$+1.90$ Dice pp in the Public-5 macro, but only BUSI and COVID-19 have
dataset-level intervals excluding zero. Target-derived captions therefore make
this a concentrated privileged synthetic referring effect rather than
clinical-report understanding.

The result is consistent with the broader paired-augmentation literature rather
than a priority claim. Augment the Pairs rewrites predefined caption keywords
during horizontal flips, while RBTCA builds a region-grounded modality-aware
representation before augmentation \cite{augmentpairs,rbtca}. Our added evidence
is chiefly diagnostic: a deterministic exact-plan no-rewrite comparison shows
that semantic correction need not improve overlap accuracy even when the shared
augmentation policy does.

The prompt-design evidence from DescriptorMedSAM and the explicit spatial
reasoning in SSA reinforce that location language can be useful when it is both
informative and correctly grounded \cite{descriptormedsam,ssa}. NTP-MRISeg
further shows that text can be integrated through a unified generative objective
rather than a conditional decoder \cite{ntpmriseg}. Their published values are
not inserted into our quantitative tables because their datasets, prompt
construction, splits, architectures, and evaluation recipes are not identical to
Protocol V3. Accordingly, they contextualize the design space but do not serve as
same-protocol evidence for or against \methodname{}.

The BRISC result requires particular care because duplicate sanitation changes
the effective sample count. The sanitation rule is fixed before training and
applied to all compared methods.

\subsection{Limitations and next evidence}
First, the V3 hash identifies fixed data and evaluation choices, not the complete
training policy. The richer R11 shared plan changes the original dataset routing
and adds affine, translation, and zoom, so the 90-cell matrix is a frozen
follow-up ablation rather than a strict execution of the original July 10
augmentation specification. Existing run-configuration and code hashes preserve
the identity of the executed batch, but the recorded Git commit omits several
orchestration/evaluation files and the currently available release copies do not
reproduce the aggregate execution-code digest. Exact clean-clone reconstruction
of the 90-cell source snapshot is therefore not demonstrated. The original
completion digest also omitted command-line fields such as batch size and worker
count; archived command logs are needed to recover them. Second, four ATConv
modules are present in checkpoint metadata,
but only the two projector modules enter the released forward graph. The reported
parameter count includes the two dormant neck modules, and no ATConv4 claim is
made. Third, the exact three-seed rewrite control shows small heterogeneous
effects and a macro bootstrap interval spanning zero. BRISC has a small negative
paired-case interval, and the grouped COVID-19 sensitivity is also negative;
neither analysis supports an accuracy-benefit claim for rewriting. Fourth, the
current framework audits reliability at dataset and checkpoint level but does
not contain a learned per-case confidence gate or automatic image-only fallback.
Fifth, continuous translation and zoom in the reported \methodname{} results do not recompute coarse
location labels. The completed three-seed \locrecompute{} diagnostic decreases
BUSI performance and is neutral on ClinicDB, but it is mask derived and does not
share exact realized transform draws with the main configuration; it cannot
establish the effect of a clinically supplied continuous-language update. Sixth,
the public prompts contain attributes extracted from the target masks or labels.
This controlled referring benchmark is useful for testing equivariance, but it
gives image+text methods privileged target metadata and cannot establish clinical
utility or a fair deployment advantage over image-only models. The post hoc
stratified derangement controls only coarse presence and diagnostic
class derived from released identifiers/prompts; it does not match morphology,
location, wording template, or other target-derived attributes and therefore
cannot isolate clinical language grounding. None of the prompts were authored
prospectively by clinicians. Seventh, the
component study is complete but is an ordered ablation rather than a
full crossing of every augmentation-policy interaction. Eighth, although six image-only architectures are evaluated with
complete three-seed coverage, architectural recipes are not forced to share input
size, loss, or augmentation; the comparison is controlled at the data and
evaluation levels rather than as an identical-training ablation. The broad model
table is therefore contextual, with rankings separated by modality. Ninth, the
five public datasets differ in modality, sample count, prompt diversity, and
spatial-language prevalence, so an aggregate average would obscure meaningful
heterogeneity. Tenth, the public benchmark manifests do not generally expose
patient or acquisition-sequence identifiers. The fixed test folders are
image-disjoint, and the recoverable COVID-19 subject IDs do not cross into test,
but patient-level independence cannot be established for every dataset from the
released metadata. For COVID-19, the grouped train--validation sensitivity
removes all overlap among 2,130 recoverable subject tokens and changes
\methodname{} Dice by only $-0.19$ pp, but 2,951 rows without recoverable tokens
remain case-level groups. The reported-checkpoint subject-cluster sensitivity
preserves the direction of the large U-Net++ and shuffled-prompt contrasts,
while the small rewrite contrast remains uncertain; comparable
clustered inference is not possible for the other public datasets from the
released metadata. ClinicDB sequence independence also cannot be verified because
sequence identifiers are absent.

The current manuscript therefore limits its primary claim to a fixed-data-and-evaluation
benchmark association led by the shared training policy. Prospective clinician-authored text and independent
cross-domain testing remain future validation rather than prerequisites for the
reported benchmark comparison.
Complete boundary-metric tables, model complexity, and qualitative failure
panels are provided in the supplement and main manuscript.

\section{Conclusions}
This study provides a 90-cell, three-seed audit of shared augmentation and
privileged prompts in multimodal medical image segmentation. The complete
configuration achieves Public-5 macro Dice of $87.87\%$ and IoU of $80.97\%$ and
differs from the horizontal-flip Base reference by $+1.46$ Dice pp (95\% CI $+0.95$ to
$+1.99$) and $+1.74$ IoU pp (95\% CI $+1.13$ to $+2.34$). The shared-plan
no-rewrite row is numerically best, and the complete configuration differs from
the post hoc Base-plus-plan control by only $+0.04$ Dice pp (95\% CI $-0.40$ to
$+0.41$). The supported accuracy claim is therefore attached primarily to the
shared follow-up training policy, not to rewriting or to an ATConv4 mechanism.
Case-specific target-derived text affects aggregate predictions after coarse
presence/class matching, but the effect is markedly attenuated and does not
establish clinical-report understanding. The protocol boundary, effective
two-projector ATConv graph, negative rewrite evidence, and privileged-prompt
control are part of the result, not implementation details to be hidden from
interpretation.
